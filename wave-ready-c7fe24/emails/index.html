ADo you need a second Kit account? (Short answer: no.)

You can run both Wave Ready and Dreamland from your one Dreamland Kit account. Here's the trade-off, then my recommendation.

What Kit allows (verified): Kit is subscriber-centric — one master list, separated by tags and segments. You can add multiple "From" addresses (add each, confirm it, then pick the right one per sequence/broadcast). So you can send Wave Ready emails from a Wave Ready address and Dreamland emails from the Dreamland address in the same account.

The one caveat: Kit authenticates a single sending domain for the whole account, and that domain shows in the technical headers of every email regardless of the "From" address. So Wave Ready emails would carry Dreamland's sending domain under the hood. Almost no recipient ever sees that (it's in the "signed-by/via" headers), and Wave Ready openly discloses the Dreamland relationship anyway — so it's a minor cosmetic thing, not a dealbreaker.

Recommendation — one account (Option A). Keep everything in the Dreamland Kit account. Add a Wave Ready From-name + address; tag every quiz subscriber wave-ready plus their result tier and goals; send the Wave Ready sequences from the Wave Ready address; never use "All Subscribers" (it mixes the two audiences — always send to the tag/segment). It's cheaper, simpler, and it's the "both brands under Dreamland" you wanted.

When a second account (Option B) is worth it: only if Wave Ready gets big and you want fully separate deliverability reputation + its own authenticated wave-ready domain, or the shared-domain header bugs you. Cost: a second subscription, double admin, and a Zap to copy converts back to Dreamland. Not worth it to start.

So when/where does the cutover to Dreamland marketing happen?

Two moments — and you should not silently migrate everyone (that erodes the third-party trust and invites spam complaints):

  1. Behavioral (implicit): the moment a subscriber clicks a Dreamland tour link or books, auto-tag them dreamland-warm and add them to Dreamland's main nurture. They've raised their hand.
  2. Bridge (explicit opt-in): near the end of each Wave Ready sequence, one hand-off email invites them to join Dreamland's list. One click. Full copy is the last email below.
The rule: Wave Ready owns the top of funnel and the help. Dreamland owns them once they click, book, or opt in. The bridge email + behavioral tagging is the cutover — no second account required.

Preview: A pattern worth knowing before you go.

Four people have died at The Wave in July heat since 2013: a couple aged 70 and 69 in July 2013, a 27-year-old hiker who lost the trail on the walk back that same month, and a 49-year-old hiker in July 2018 who still had water with him.

Subject: Going on your own: the short version

Preview: Five things to get right on the day.

Hi {{first_name}}, based on your answers you could hike The Wave on your own. Here's the short version of doing it right:

  1. Enter both lotteries — the advance one (~4 months out) and the daily geofence one (2 days before).
  2. Download the BLM landmark-photo route sheet and an offline GPS track. Study the photos before you go.
  3. Be walking by first light. Carry a gallon of water, salty snacks, and a hard turnaround time.
  4. Carry a satellite messenger. Cell service is spotty at the trailhead and mostly absent on the route.
  5. A normal high-clearance vehicle is fine for the trailhead when the road's dry; skip it if it's been wet.

Conditions on the day decide, so check the forecast and the road the night before, and turn back if anything feels wrong.

Preview: Melody Arch, the Second Wave, and 190 million years of story.

You'll get the famous shot. But the Wave rewards people who know where to look:

  • Melody Arch — guests say it rivals the Wave itself. It's past a steep slickrock climb and isn't visible from below, so most people miss it. The challenge is route-finding, not nerve.

  • The rock itself — petrified sand dunes; the colors are iron and manganese, and the dark crust between the rock is alive (step only on stone).

Your plan has notes on finding these. If you'd rather have someone read the whole landscape for you, that's the one thing a guide adds even for strong hikers — more on that later.

Subject: Let's get you a permit (or a great Plan B)

Preview: Both lotteries, the deadlines, and your odds.

The Wave lets in 64 people a day, and fewer than 1 in 10 applicants win. So the smart play is to enter both lotteries and have a backup you'd actually be happy with.

  • Advance lottery: apply ~4 months ahead, any device.
  • Daily geofence lottery: apply on your phone, inside the geofence, 2 days before.

To hike in a given month, enter the online lottery four months earlier: for April, enter during December. Daily lottery winners must be at the Kanab or Page permit office at 8:30 the next morning for a safety briefing. Full mechanics here → How the Wave permit works

Subject: When the light is best at the Wave

Preview: Mid-morning for the Wave, afternoon for Melody Arch.

The Wave itself photographs best in the mid-morning hours, when the sun is high enough that no shadow falls across the formation. Afternoon light suits Melody Arch and the side features.

Start hiking at first light either way. In hot months, finish your photos by late morning and start back before the heat. If you stay for late light, carry a headlamp, save your return track and set a turnaround time.
